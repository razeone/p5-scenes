## How to read this list

These references fall into two categories, marked per entry:

* **Documented influence** — a well-established, widely cited connection between the filmmaker's visual approach and Renaissance/Baroque religious painting traditions (including chiaroscuro and tableau staging), as discussed in film criticism and the filmmakers' own commentary.
* **Stylistic parallel** — no claimed direct citation of this specific painting; included because the technique or theme closely parallels an element in [transfiguration-raphael.md](./transfiguration-raphael.md), and is useful as a visual mood reference.

## Direct biblical/tableau staging

* **The Gospel According to St. Matthew (1964), dir. Pier Paolo Pasolini** — *Documented influence.* Pasolini staged his Christ narrative using deliberately painterly, static tableaux, openly drawing on Italian Renaissance and Mannerist religious painting for composition and gesture. A strong reference for translating a two-dimensional devotional composition into blocked, camera-ready staging.
* **The Passion of the Christ (2004), dir. Mel Gibson, cinematography by Caleb Deschanel** — *Documented influence.* Deschanel has spoken about referencing Caravaggio's chiaroscuro for the film's lighting design — hard single-source light, deep shadow, and dramatic tenebrism applied to a Christian narrative subject.
* **Andrei Rublev (1966), dir. Andrei Tarkovsky** — *Documented influence.* Built around the life of an icon painter; Tarkovsky's compositions and use of light frequently echo the tableau logic of religious painting and iconography.
* **The Seventh Seal (1957), dir. Ingmar Bergman** — *Stylistic parallel.* Allegorical, high-contrast staging of divine/mortal confrontation; useful for the "elevated/transcendent figure vs. suffering mortals in the same frame" idea.

## Chiaroscuro and single-source lighting traditions

* **Barry Lyndon (1975), dir. Stanley Kubrick, cinematography by John Alcott** — *Documented influence.* Extensively referenced Dutch and English portraiture and used candlelight-only photography (including specially adapted lenses) to replicate period painting light.
* **The Godfather (1972), dir. Francis Ford Coppola, cinematography by Gordon Willis** — *Documented influence.* Willis is widely cited as drawing on Rembrandt-style top-lighting and heavy shadow, nicknamed "The Prince of Darkness" for his low-key lighting choices.
* **The Conformist (1970) and Apocalypse Now (1979), cinematography by Vittorio Storaro** — *Documented influence.* Storaro has spoken extensively about deliberate, symbolic use of primary colors and light/dark contrast, comparable to the faith/hope/love color coding in the painting's upper register.

## Vertical/two-register composition and transcendence imagery

* **The Tree of Life (2011), dir. Terrence Malick** — *Stylistic parallel.* Explicitly stages a contrast between cosmic/transcendent imagery (light, elevation, wide open space) and grounded human suffering/family drama — a close thematic cousin to the painting's upper/lower register split.
* **2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), dir. Stanley Kubrick** — *Stylistic parallel.* The Star Child sequence stages transcendence/rebirth through pure light and elevation, a useful visual analog for the painting's radiant, weightless Christ figure.
* **Noah (2014) and Mother! (2017), dir. Darren Aronofsky** — *Stylistic parallel.* Both stage large-scale biblical/allegorical narratives with a contrast between divine intervention and chaotic human crowds — relevant for crowd-blocking the lower register's apostles and family group.

## Painterly tableau and art-historical staging

* **The Draughtsman's Contract (1982) and other films by Peter Greenaway** — *Documented influence.* Greenaway is an art historian by training and consistently builds frames as direct compositional quotations of period painting, including deliberate tableau vivant staging.
* **Kundun (1997) and Silence (2016), dir. Martin Scorsese** — *Stylistic parallel.* Both use religious iconography, symmetrical staging, and controlled, painterly lighting for scenes of spiritual crisis and revelation.
